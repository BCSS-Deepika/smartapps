Why the architecture matters

A sentiment dashboard answers what. Intelligence has to answer why.

SAGA is not a social media monitoring tool, a sentiment dashboard, a social listening platform, a keyword tracker or a reputation application. It combines those into one intelligence ecosystem — which only works if the engine beneath them is shared.

Conversations move faster than reports

A claim, a grievance or a controversy crosses platforms in hours. A weekly report arrives after the damage is done.

The signals sit in different places

Social platforms, news portals, forums, review sites and client data each arrive in their own format, language and access method.

Every finding has to stand up

Sources, timestamps, hashes and analyst actions must be preserved, because an AI assessment is not evidence on its own.

The platform connects all three: see the signal, understand the narrative, decide the response.

Collection depth

Three tiers of the web, one collection layer.

Most monitoring tools read only what a search engine already indexes. SAGA collects across three distinct tiers — surface, deep and dark — each with its own retrieval method, its own authorisation requirement and its own controls. All three converge on the same normalisation layer, so a finding carries the same provenance regardless of where it came from.

T1 Surface
web

Surface web — indexed and openly reachable

Everything a standard search engine can already reach: public pages, public profiles and openly published content. This is the volume tier — it sets the baseline the other two are measured against.

What it covers

  • Public social posts, pages and comments
  • News portals and agency wires
  • Blogs, forums and public-domain pages
  • Consumer review and feedback sites
  • Published government and institutional pages

How it is retrieved

  • Social platforms: official platform APIs or licensed data providers only
  • News and open websites: scheduled collection of permitted pages
  • RSS and syndication feeds
  • Licensed aggregator feeds
  • Continuous, high-volume collection

Controls that apply

  • No scraping of social platforms; platform terms followed
  • Open websites: site terms and robots directives respected
  • Rate and quota limits enforced per source
  • Deduplication before analysis
  • Source, URL and timestamp captured on every item
Narrative detectionSentiment baselineTrend & momentumMedia monitoringReview intelligence
Below this line nothing is indexed — retrieval becomes query-driven and authorisation-bound
T2 Deep
web

Deep web — reachable, but not indexed

Content that exists publicly or under licence but sits behind a search form, a login the client holds, a paywall or a database query, so no crawler returns it. This is the corroboration tier: it is where a claim seen on the surface is checked against a record.

What it covers

  • Public records and registry portals that require a query
  • Paginated databases and archive systems
  • Subscription and paywalled media held under licence
  • Authorised government and institutional feeds
  • Client-provided datasets and contracted intelligence feeds
  • Unindexed but publicly reachable pages

How it is retrieved

  • Query-driven retrieval, never blanket crawling
  • Parameterised lookups against permitted portals
  • Licensed feed ingestion
  • Credentials the client owns and authorises, held in their custody
  • Scoped to the mandate of the engagement

Controls that apply

  • Written authorisation recorded per source
  • Purpose limitation — queries tied to a case or mandate
  • No access to private accounts or personal inboxes
  • Query, operator and result written to the audit log
  • Retention set by contract
Entity corroborationRecord verificationEvidence supportB2B review sourcesHistorical archives
T3 Dark
web

Dark web — overlay networks, authorised engagements only

Sites reachable only through overlay networks such as Tor or I2P. This is the early-warning tier: it exists to tell a client that something of theirs has surfaced where it should not have. Collection is read-only and the platform never participates in what it observes.

What it covers

  • Publicly listed leak and data-dump advertisements
  • Paste sites and public indexes on overlay networks
  • Open forum and marketplace listings visible without membership
  • Mentions of a client's domains, brands, officials or assets
  • Credential-exposure indicators for client-owned assets only, handled under the client's mandate

How it is retrieved

  • Isolated, hardened collection environment separated from all other infrastructure
  • Read-only monitoring of openly visible listings
  • Keyword and entity matching against the client's declared assets
  • Indicators extracted; source material is not republished
  • Enabled per engagement, not by default

Controls that apply

  • Legal sign-off and a defined mandate before the tier is switched on
  • Jurisdiction checked for the deployment
  • No transactions, no membership, no contact with operators
  • Illegal material is filtered at collection, not retained and not displayed; any incident is escalated to legal
  • Strict retention limits and full audit of every collection run

Scoring methodology

Goodwill is a measurement, so it needs a method.

The name is Sentiment And Goodwill Analysis, so goodwill has to be more than positive minus negative. The conceptual model below is the product direction; weighting, normalisation and validation must be formally established before any numerical index is published to a client.

A composite index, not a sentiment average. Each input is measured separately so a score can be explained, challenged and reproduced.

Alert model

Not every negative comment is an alert.

Priority bands are configurable per vertical, and every band rests on defined risk indicators with human review — never on negative sentiment alone.

Critical

A credible, imminent threat requiring urgent review and escalation.

High

Medium

Low

Data protection & compliance

Personal data handled to Indian law, in the client's jurisdiction.

Public content can contain personal data, and client datasets often do. SAGA is designed to support the client's obligations under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025, which come into force in phases through May 2027.

Purpose limitation

Every workspace, watchlist and query is tied to a recorded purpose and mandate. Data collected for one purpose is not reused for another without a new authorisation.

Data minimisation

Only the fields a stated purpose needs are kept. Identifiers that analysis does not need are masked or dropped at ingestion.

Consent and lawful basis

Client-provided personal data, such as CRM or complaint records, is processed only on the basis the client records: consent, or another use the Act permits.

Data residency

Stored and processed in India by default: in the client's chosen cloud region or in their own data centre. No transfer outside India without written approval.

Retention and erasure

Retention is set per source and workspace by contract, with deletion at expiry. Correction and erasure requests run through the client's grievance process.

Breach response

Security incidents are logged and reported to the client, with the records the client needs to notify the Data Protection Board and affected individuals.

Applies to client-provided data and to any personal data that appears in collected public content. Each deployment's position under the Act is confirmed with the client's legal team before go-live.

Deployment models

Cloud, private cloud, on-premise or air-gapped.

The same platform runs in four ways. The choice follows the client's data classification, not the vertical, and it can change as an engagement moves from pilot to production.

Managed cloud

Run by Blue Cloud in an India cloud region. Fastest to start; suited to brand, celebrity and pilot engagements.

Private cloud

Dedicated tenancy in the client's own cloud account or an approved government cloud, on an isolated network, with keys the client holds.

On-premise

Installed in the client's data centre. All collected data, models and logs stay on client infrastructure; support works under the client's access controls.

Air-gapped

The analysis environment has no internet connection. Collection runs in a separate environment and data enters through a controlled, logged transfer. For classified police and government use.

Tenant isolation and data segregation
Separate data per client

Each client's data sits in its own logically separate store with its own encryption keys. On private cloud, on-premise and air-gapped deployments the separation is physical.

Separate workspaces

Inside one client, verticals, departments and cases are separate workspaces with their own users, watchlists and retention.

No cross-client learning

One client's data, labels and analyst corrections are not used to train models that serve another client.

AI governance

Models are versioned, measured and corrected by analysts.

AI output is decision support, so the models behind it are held to the same scrutiny as the findings. This matters most in the political workspace.

Model versioning

Every finding records the model and version that produced it. Model changes go out through a change log, with rollback.

Analyst feedback loop

Analysts correct sentiment, entity and classification labels on screen. Corrections are reviewed, then used to recalibrate and retrain.

Accuracy monitoring

Accuracy is measured per language and per vertical against labelled samples, and reported to the client. A language goes live only after it meets the agreed threshold.

Bias review

Scoring is reviewed for skew across parties, regions, languages and communities before each release, and on a fixed schedule after it.

Explainable scores

Every score shows the posts and factors behind it, so an analyst can say why a number moved.

Human sign-off

No automated action is taken against a person. Escalations, reports and evidence packages are signed by a named officer.

Built for how intelligence work is scrutinised

Fast for the analyst. Defensible for the organisation.

Lawful collection only

Publicly accessible and authorised sources. Social data comes through official platform APIs or licensed providers, within platform terms and applicable law.

Every action logged

Searches, scans, results and sign-offs are recorded with the analyst who performed them.

Similarity is not attribution

The platform can show that two accounts look related. It cannot establish who owns one.

A human decides

AI output is decision support. Negative sentiment is never treated as evidence of an offence.

Not a poll

Online discussion is not representative sampling and is never presented as voting intention.

Provenance preserved

Source, timestamp and hash travel with the finding, because an AI assessment is not admissible alone.

No profiling of private citizens

SAGA reads public conversation in aggregate. It does not build profiles of private individuals or record a citizen's political views.

Not a targeting tool

It is never used to target, persuade or act against individuals for political ends, including micro-targeting of voters.

One method for every party

Models, scoring and alert thresholds are applied identically across parties, leaders and regions, and checked for skew.
