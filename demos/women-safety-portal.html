Why this exists

The record exists. It is never in one place at the right time.

The person is scattered

The FIR sits in CCTNS, the trial in e-Courts, the sentence in e-Prisons. One individual across three cases becomes three unconnected records, and the pattern never surfaces.

NOW · ONE MASTER PROFILE, MANY LINKED CASES

Release passes unnoticed

A convicted offender completes a sentence and returns to a jurisdiction where no officer was told. The information existed — it never reached the station that needed it.

NOW · RELEASE EVENTS RAISE A ROUTED ALERT

Employers have no lawful route

A school hiring a bus driver has documents and references and no proper way to ask the police anything — so either nothing is checked, or records move through channels that should not exist.

NOW · ONE CONSENTED, AUDITED, GATED CHANNEL
Two Operational Ecosystems

Same platform. Separate access. Identical limit.

Police use the registry to monitor and manage cases and individuals under lawful authority. Every other requester — school, university, childcare body, hospital or corporation — uses a different door that returns an outcome, never a record.

DOOR A · LAWFUL AUTHORITY

Police & women safety team operations

The operational registry: identify, register and track individuals connected to sexual-offence cases, with legal status maintained accurately at every stage.

  • Consolidated registry of case-linked individuals
  • Accused, under-trial and convicted as distinct categories
  • Release, bail and legal-status change alerts
  • Verified addresses, geotagged and jurisdiction-resolved
  • Repeat-offender and multi-case identification
  • women safety team field assignments and follow-up history

DOOR B · CONTROLLED DISCLOSURE

Institutional & employer verification

A segregated portal where registered requesters submit consented background-verification requests for people in roles with access to women and children — and receive an outcome against a defined scope.

  • Requester registration and authentication
  • Consent and authorisation captured with every request
  • Requests routed to the correct police jurisdiction
  • Authorised officer reviews before any response is issued
  • Outcome returned as a referenced verification certificate
  • Full request and access history retained for audit

No requester goes beyond the limit — corporations included

There is no enhanced tier. A corporation is held to exactly the same gate, the same scope and the same outcome set as a primary school, whatever its size, sector, contract value or stated reason. Size does not buy access, and neither does a commercial relationship with the department. Door B returns one of five outcomes and nothing else.

Safeguards

Useful to the officer. Fair to the individual.

Victim identity protected

Victim information is restricted throughout and never leaves through Door B, reflecting the statutory bar on disclosing a victim's identity and the added safeguards for child victims.

Due process preserved

Accused, under-trial and convicted stay separate in the data model, the workflows and every disclosure. A pending FIR is never returned as a finding.

Access is narrow and logged

Role-based access by rank, unit and jurisdiction, with an immutable record of every view, search, download and disclosure, exportable for vigilance or court.

Consent and a human reviewer

No verification runs without recorded consent, and no response is issued without an authorised officer deciding what is disclosable.

Provenance on every field

Each record carries its source system, verification status and last-updated time, so an officer sees how current and how reliable it is.

Retention and correction

Approved retention, correction and deletion policies apply, with a legal and data-protection review against the applicable framework.

Monitoring within authority

Geotagging an address is not live tracking, and release is not surveillance. Each assignment records the order or procedure it acts on.

Structural separation

Door B has no path to the registry, the map or the case record. The boundary is in the architecture, not in a permission setting someone can widen.

Before Build

Four decisions that shape the architecture.

Each of these changes the data model or the workflow engine, so they are worth settling with the department before development rather than during it.

DECISION 01

Registry coverage

Does the registry hold only sexual-offence records, or also linked trafficking, child exploitation and related offences?

→ Sets the classification engine's inclusion rules
DECISION 02

Release monitoring scope

Is monitoring limited to convicted individuals released from prison, or does it also cover accused persons released on bail, under a separate workflow?

→ Sets the alert engine and the legal-status rules
DECISION 03

Disclosable record definition

Which legal statuses are ever disclosable through Door B? The gate assumes conviction-based disclosure only.

→ Recommended: conviction-based, with a named reviewing officer
DECISION 04

Location requirement

Is the requirement address geotagging with officer field verification, or is approved live-location tracking also in scope?

→ Determines whether a device integration is needed at all
